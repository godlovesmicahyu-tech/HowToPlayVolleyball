How To Play Volleyball Like A Pro!

Introduction

Volleyball is a very diverse sport that people of all sizes, athletic-levels, and ages can play! The goal of players is to get the ball up when attacked and to make the ball touch the ground on the enemy's side. They have three touches on the ball to succeed in doing this. While some positions in volleyball are better suited for some than others, every person can practice to be the best they can at every aspect of volleyball. The parts of this sport can be broken down into five positions: the server, receiver, setter, spiker, and blocker Lets start by examining the aspect of volleyball that starts all the other actions off, that being, the serve.

Table of Contents:

1. The serve 2. The receive 3. The set 4. The spike 5. The block 6. Repeat



The Serve

The serve starts off all other plays or actions in volleyball. When serving, there are a couple levels to what you can do. Most people start off with the underhanded serve. When they feel ready, they can switch to a flat, overhead serve. After that, they can switch to a jump float serve. Finally, there is the top-spin jump serve.


The Receive

After the serve, there must be a receive. A player will move to intercept the ball before it hits the ground and attempt to bump or overhead set it to the setter. When they receive, they try to get the ball high, with as little spin as possible, so that the setter will have less difficulty to set it.


The Set

Then, once the ball has been received, the setter will move to the ball and set the ball to the player that they think will be able to score. The set can vary in height and speed depending on what the spiker prefers or the setter has decided to do. The different sets are assigned numbers, colors, and even hand-signs depending on the team.


The Spike

After the ball has been set, the spiker comes into play. They first start by backing up once the ball is received and will start their apporach depending on the type of set they are running. While they are backing up and approaching, they are also watching where the blockers and receivers are currently at. Then, they jump and either spike or tip the ball to score a point.


The Block

Obviously the opposing team's defenders are not going to just let the spikers have a free chance at scoring. While the front row defenders are waiting for the setter to set the ball, they are watching for which spiker might be used to attack. They can either move after the ball is set, guess block by moving to the person they think most likely to spike, or block by staying on one particular spiker.


Repeat The Receive

If the blockers fail to stop the spiker's attack, the process repeats. The receivers get to the ball, the setter sets it, the spiker spikes it, and the blockers try to stop it. That being said, these aren't the only possible outcomes for volleyball! The process can be interupted if the receiver fails to get the ball up or if the setter chooses to do a surprise attack by dumping the ball on the enemy's court. The spiker might also hit the ball on second touch. Volleyball is a very diverse sport that is able to have limitless outcomes or strategies making for a very fun time for anyone who chooses to play it.
